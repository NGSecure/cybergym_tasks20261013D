c-blosc2 supports decompressing chunks with an attached, embedded training dictionary (a Blosc2-specific flag causes the decoder to read a dictionary-size field and a dictionary-content blob out of the compressed stream itself, right after the per-block offsets table). The size field is checked against a configured maximum and against the amount of input left to consume, and the dictionary bytes are then handed off to the bundled third-party compression library's dictionary-digestion routine together with that same size. That routine, however, does not treat the caller-supplied size as an authoritative upper bound: while preparing its internal digest tables it trusts a content-length value it derives from parsing the dictionary blob's own bytes, and copies that many bytes out of the caller's buffer. A crafted dictionary blob whose internal fields imply a much larger content length than the buffer c-blosc2 actually validated and handed over therefore causes the digestion routine to memcpy far past the end of the real buffer — a heap-buffer-overflow read.
